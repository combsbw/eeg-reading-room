The systematic 12-lead read
  1. Technical. Calibration, speed, filter, artifact, lead reversal.
  2. Rate. 300 ÷ large boxes, or complexes in 10 s × 6.
  3. Rhythm. Regular? P before every QRS? QRS after every P? Constant PR?
  4. Axis. Leads I and aVF, then II.
  5. Intervals. PR, QRS, QT and QTc.
  6. P waves. Atrial enlargement, ectopic or retrograde P.
  7. QRS. Q waves, voltage, R-wave progression, bundle-branch patterns.
  8. ST segments. Elevation, depression, territory, reciprocity, equivalents.
  9. T and U waves. Inverted, hyperacute, peaked, flat, U waves.
  10. Synthesis. Compare with old ECGs, correlate clinically, repeat when in doubt.

Pick a mode and press Start

Diagnosis questions ask for the main finding and follow up with a culprit-artery or management question where it matters. Measurement questions ask for rate, regularity, axis, PR, QRS or QTc, computed from the tracing itself. Practice adapts to the patterns you miss; Exam runs fifteen questions and reviews them at the end. Change the layout, gain, speed or filter on any tracing, and drag across it to use calipers.

Tracings are computer-generated teaching models built from a vector model of the heart, not patient recordings. Real ECGs vary more, and nothing here is for clinical decisions. Thresholds and treatments summarize common guidance and change over time; follow your local protocols and medical direction.
