A systematic way to read a page
  1. Technical. Montage, sensitivity, filters, patient state and age.
  2. Background. Posterior rhythm frequency, symmetry, reactivity; the front-to-back gradient.
  3. Slowing. Focal or generalized, polymorphic or rhythmic.
  4. Epileptiform. Aftergoing slow wave, physiologic field, disruption of background.
  5. Periodic or rhythmic patterns. Lateralized or generalized, rate, evolution.
  6. Artifact. Is it confined to one electrode, locked to the ECG, or high-frequency?
  7. Localize. Phase reversals in bipolar, field in referential.
  8. Impression. Normal or abnormal, degree, distribution, clinical question.

Pick a mode and press Start

Practice shows an explanation after every answer and adapts to the patterns you miss. Exam runs ten different patterns and shows the review at the end. You can change montage, sensitivity and filters on any tracing, and drag across it to measure time and frequency.

Tracings are computer-generated teaching models, not patient recordings. Real EEG is messier and more variable, and nothing here is for clinical interpretation. Few EEG patterns are strictly pathognomonic; the abnormal examples are the classic or highly characteristic ones, and each carries caveats about context. Negativity is plotted upward, as is the convention.
